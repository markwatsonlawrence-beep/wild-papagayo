Why visit Monteverde?

Monteverde is one of Costa Rica's major ecotourism destinations, centered on the Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve, which National Geographic called "the jewel in the crown of cloud forest reserves" and which Costa Ricans voted one of the nation's seven natural wonders. Its tropical montane cloud forest, cool climate, and rich biodiversity draw naturalists and travelers from around the world.

The region also carries a unique cultural heritage, having been founded by Quakers from the United States in the 1950s who set aside land for conservation. Today the town of Santa Elena serves as a welcoming hub with diverse accommodations, dining, and guided tours, and English-Spanish bilingualism is widespread.

What travelers usually like

  • Monteverde Cloud Forest Biological Reserve, the largest of the area's reserves
  • Voted one of Costa Rica's seven natural wonders
  • Cool cloud-forest climate with a mean annual temperature around 18°C (64°F)
  • Santa Elena, the tourist hub for lodging, dining, and guided tours
  • Unique Quaker heritage and widespread English-Spanish bilingualism

Things to consider

  • The area receives an estimated 250,000 tourists a year, so popular sites can draw considerable crowds.
  • Monteverde is roughly a four-hour drive from the Central Valley and roads have only been partially paved in recent years.
  • Annual precipitation averages around 3,000 mm and humidity is high, so expect frequent mist and rain.

Frequently asked questions

What is Monteverde known for?

Monteverde is famous for the Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve, described by National Geographic as "the jewel in the crown of cloud forest reserves" and voted one of Costa Rica's seven natural wonders.

Where is Monteverde and how far is it from the Central Valley?

Monteverde is located in the Cordillera de Tilarán in Puntarenas province and is roughly a four-hour drive from the Central Valley.

What is the climate like in Monteverde?

Monteverde has a tropical montane cloud forest climate at about 1,400 m elevation. It is never hot thanks to Caribbean trade winds, with a mean annual temperature around 18°C (64°F), high rainfall, and humidity between 74% and 97%.

Wildlife You May See

Resplendent Quetzal

Moves seasonally from high-elevation nesting sites to lower elevations.

Source: Wikipedia: Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve

Three-wattled Bellbird

Breeds near the Continental Divide March-June, then moves downhill to the Pacific slope in August-September.

Source: Wikipedia: Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve

Swallow-tailed Kite

Breeds in Monteverde, migrates to South America in the non-reproductive phase.

Source: Wikipedia: Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve

Golden Toad

Endemic species, last seen in 1989 -- now believed extinct due to chytrid fungus.

Source: Wikipedia: Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve
